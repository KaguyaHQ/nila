# Design Philosophy

The product principles every screen answers to. This is the bridge between
[`BRAND.md`](./BRAND.md) — *why* Nila feels like the moon — and
[`DESIGN.md`](./DESIGN.md) — *how* the moonlight system is built. Brand sets
the mood; this sets the priorities; the design system renders them. Read it
before deciding what goes on a screen and in what order.

Nila replaces seven inboxes and a 154-tab spreadsheet for a back office that
runs at a desktop. Its one job is to turn operational chaos into calm. The user
comes to get paid and keep the business moving; compliance rides underneath,
unseen. Design every screen to that end — and let beauty be how Nila wins once
the work is right, never a substitute for it.

## Lead with the money

The first thing on any screen is what's owed and what's blocking it — not a
balance, not a feed, not a score. The question that wakes this business up is
where is my money and what do I do to get it. Answer it before it's asked.

## Compliance is invisible

The user never performs a "compliance task." GST correctness shows up as the
invoice simply being right, assembled from the operational data already flowing
through. If anyone has to go do compliance inside Nila, the design failed.

## Arrive with the work done

Nila is a colleague who already did the prep — not a blank form to fill, not a
bot to prompt. Records come pre-filled from the documents that created them;
screens exist for reviewing and correcting, not transcribing. The default state
of a record is already populated.

## Propose, then confirm

Trust is the entire product, and it's fragile. Nothing that moves money or
reaches a customer fires without a human tap. Nila always shows its work — what
it matched on, why it flagged something, how sure it is. A guess never looks
like a fact.

## Prevent the error; don't report it

Every mistake in this business is administrative, not a judgment call — wrong
tax type, HSN mismatch, missing place of supply. Catch it at the point of
creation, before the document leaves. Reporting an error after rejection is just
a faster way to learn we failed.

## Nothing is filler

In a dense domain, this is ruthless prioritization. Show the number that
matters; defer the rest. A screen that shows everything shows nothing.

## Remove before adding

When a screen feels unclear, subtract first. Empty space is better than a noisy
control that exists for a hypothetical case. Add UI only when it serves a real
workflow the user already has.

## Organization comes from the design itself

Do not explain the interface with labels the layout should have made obvious.
Grouping, order, hierarchy, and spacing should tell the user what belongs
together. A screen that needs too much instructional copy is usually organized
wrong.

## Every pixel is intentional

Spacing, alignment, color, and typography should feel considered, not default.
If two elements look slightly off, fix it. Users feel it even if they can't name
it.

## Quiet until needed

Surface the outstanding amount; reveal the line-item GST breakdown only when
someone is reviewing the invoice. The common case should feel effortless; the
rare case should be reachable, not resident.

## Things are what they are

A draft looks like a draft until it's confirmed; a button that sends, sends. An
irreversible action looks like a last resort — never an equal option sitting
beside a safe one. Visual weight matches consequence.

## Controls move toward the user's intent

Actions should describe the outcome the user wants, not the workaround required
to get there. Prefer "Approve invoice" over "Clear exception"; prefer "Send
reminder" over "Mark not followed up." The product should reduce translation
work, especially on repeated operational tasks.

## Own the full experience

If a flow starts in Nila, it should conclude in Nila unless an external system is
the actual system of record. Every handoff to email, a portal, a spreadsheet, or
a downloaded file asks the user to hold state in their head. Before adding an
out-of-product step, ask whether Nila can keep the context and complete the job.

## Match density to the moment

Scan surfaces — the money map, lists, the daily queue — are tight and
glanceable, a row understood at once. Review surfaces — invoice, quote detail —
breathe, because that's where a human makes a money decision and needs room to
think.

## Speak the operator's language

Labels are the words they'd say out loud, never accounting-software jargon. And
numbers are the product: tabular figures that align down a column, Indian digit
grouping always (₹12,50,000).

## Calm is the aesthetic

Their world is overload; relief is what we deliver. Light, precise, legible,
unhurried — a clean ledger that does its own arithmetic. Beauty is how we win
the tie. It is never the reason we win.
